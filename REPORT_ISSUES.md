# OsdagBridge Baseline Report: Issue Audit

Audit of the baseline report generated by the OsdagBridge desktop app with default values (generated PDF, 75 physical pages, plus the generated .tex source). Method: page-by-page visual inspection of all 75 pages, plus a skim of the TeX structure. Analysis only; nothing here changes any engineering result.

**Page convention:** PDF viewer page first, printed footer page in parentheses. After the cover, printed page = PDF page minus one.

## Part 1: The six issues named in the assessment brief

### 1. Multi-page tables split without repeating column headers
- **Pages:** PDF 11 (10), 18 (17); PDF 25-28 (24-27), 30-31 (29-30), 33-35 (32-34), 37-40 (36-39); PDF 42-46 (41-45), 52 (51), 58 (57), 60-63 (59-62).
- **What is wrong:** Long tables (restraint/stiffener, load combinations, girder properties, section classification, capacity, interaction, LTB, serviceability, deck loading, bracing, diaphragm, overall summary) continue onto the next page with no repeated column headers and no "continued" caption. When a member group splits across a page break, continuation rows also lose their girder/panel identity and the table border is left open at the break (PDF 24-28 (23-27), 33-34 (32-33), 45-46 (44-45), 60-61 (59-60)).
- **Reviewer impact:** The longest tables in the report are unreadable past their first page. A reviewer cannot tell which column is which or which girder a row belongs to. This is the most visible defect class in the document.
- **Implementation note:** The generated TeX already contains 49 longtable environments, but only two define a header block, and one of those is empty. The fix is adding proper `\endhead` continuation definitions, not switching packages. The analysis maximum-demands table (PDF 19-21 (18-20)) already repeats its header correctly and can serve as the pattern.

### 2. Table rows bleed into the footer margin
- **Pages:** PDF 29 (28), 34 (33), 42 (41).
- **What is wrong:** Multirow girder labels extend below their table rows into the footer zone (G9 on PDF 29, G8 on PDF 34 and PDF 42). On PDF 34 the G8 label crosses the green footer rule.
- **Reviewer impact:** Body content colliding with the footer looks unpolished and breaks the page grid.
- **Note:** The brief's exact example (page 30, a G4 row) does not reproduce at this pagination: PDF 31 (printed 30) is a clean G6-G9 moment-table continuation. Same defect, different concrete instance.

### 3. Live Loads and Footway Load tables merged
- **Pages:** PDF 15 (14).
- **What is wrong:** Footway load appears as an empty row inside the vehicle live-load table instead of its own structured table, and dedicated unit columns are absent. Vehicle classes, impact factors and braking information are crowded into a generic two-column table instead of clearly separated fields with units.
- **Reviewer impact:** Load data looks incomplete and is hard to check against the IRC load cases.

### 4. Missing utilization-ratio bar charts (Section 5.5)
- **Pages:** PDF 62-63 (61-62), Section 5.5.
- **What is wrong:** Only tabular utilization ratios are given. There are no UR (Demand/Capacity) bar charts for girders, deck slab, cross bracing or end diaphragms, and no red dashed threshold line at UR = 1.
- **Reviewer impact:** The brief explicitly asks for these charts; the section reads as unfinished without them.

### 5. Missing material quantity bar charts (Chapter 7)
- **Pages:** PDF 70 (69), Chapter 7.
- **What is wrong:** Only a bill-of-materials table is present. Bar charts for steel tonnage, concrete volume and reinforcement weight are missing.
- **Reviewer impact:** Explicitly requested output that does not exist in the report.

### 6. Formatting hardcoded and scattered (no centralized style module)
- **Pages:** Visible as inconsistency across PDF 7-18 (6-17) versus 24-63 (23-62); the executive figures/tables on PDF 3-5 (2-4) also use a different numbering and caption style from the numbered chapters.
- **What is wrong:** Table headers, widths, padding and caption punctuation vary from chapter to chapter.
- **Limitation:** The generated output shows the inconsistency, and the TeX repeats local formatting and duplicated page-style blocks, but the absence of a centralized Python style module can only be confirmed in the codebase, not from these generated files alone.

## Part 2: Additional issues found (not in the brief)

### Data contradictions (data bugs, not styling)
These three are flagged for the technical changes document. The data source must be traced before any engineering result is changed; the report must not be silently restyled to hide the contradiction.

1. **Pass/fail contradiction between the executive summary and detailed checks.** PDF 5 (4): executive outcome text says the girder passes, although the overview (PDF 3 (2)) reports failure and the detailed deflection and fatigue checks fail (PDF 45-49 (44-48)).
2. **Deflection number mismatch.** PDF 62 (61): the overall summary reports 30.86/50 mm (UR 0.62), but the detailed total deflection reaches 70.521/50 mm and fails (PDF 22 (21), 45-46 (44-45)).
3. **Same maximum UR repeated for every girder.** PDF 5 (4): the executive table shows UR 2.14 for every girder, while the detailed per-girder values differ, roughly 1.97-2.14 (PDF 48-49 (47-48)).

### Tables and page furniture
4. **Executive girder table runs off the right edge.** PDF 5 (4): the G8 column is cut off and G9 disappears entirely.
5. **Headers and footers disappear mid-report.** PDF 45-46 (44-45): running headers, left institutional footer and footer rule are missing; the remaining right page counter is visibly cut off.
6. **Stranded table caption.** PDF 4-5 (3-4): the final-geometry table caption sits at the bottom of PDF 4 while its table starts on PDF 5.
7. **No overall verdict in the summary.** PDF 62-63 (61-62): no explicit pass/fail column or overall verdict; only the fatigue UR is red, while the contradictory deflection row looks satisfactory.
8. **Blank first-column header and inconsistent column naming.** PDF 30-46 (29-45), Tables 5.3-5.9: the first column holds girder IDs but its header is blank; parameter/formula/value/status naming varies between tables.
9. **Empty support-reaction table.** PDF 22 (21): a header plus entirely empty rows, although the preceding analysis table contains reaction results.
10. **Blank stiffener spacing cells.** PDF 41-43 (40-42): intermediate-stiffener spacing cells are blank instead of populated or explicitly marked unavailable.
11. **Diaphragm summary labels do not match the units.** PDF 63 (62): moment/shear labels although both demand and capacity are in kN; inconsistent with the axial-member capacity tables on PDF 61-62 (60-61).

### Units and unfinished fields
12. **Input girder dimensions mislabeled.** PDF 9 (8): depth 1.67 mm and web thickness 0.01 mm versus the correct later values of 1670 mm and 10 mm (PDF 24 (23), 66 (65)); flange headings and values also mix metres and millimetres.
13. **Units missing, duplicated or inconsistent.** PDF 3, 8-9, 13 (2, 7-8, 12): unitless overview spacing/thickness; units repeated in both labels and values.
14. **Unfinished load fields.** PDF 15-17 (14-16): unexplained blanks or bare units for footway, wind, seismic-force and temperature data; not-applicable and not-calculated are not distinguished.
15. **Wearing-course load not actually calculated.** PDF 15 (14): shown as material multiplied by thickness, rather than a computed load with units.

### Drawings and figures
16. **Diagonal section drawings do not match the design.** PDF 56, 59 (55, 58): the drawings repeat the 40 mm-high chord section, while the corresponding diagonal entries specify 45x45x3 (PDF 68-69 (67-68)). Image-to-design consistency needs checking.
17. **Diaphragm layout uses bracing IDs.** PDF 68 (67): B1M1-style identification instead of the diaphragm E-series IDs used in the text (PDF 13 (12)).
18. **Analysis diagrams hard to read.** PDF 22-23 (21-22): overlapping member/value labels and tiny axis text make the peaks hard to read.
19. **Drawing pages waste space.** PDF 64-69 (63-68): uneven scale and whitespace; PDF 69 contains only three small sections near the top, with most of the page empty and captions far below the drawings.
20. **Caption and annotation style.** PDF 68-69 (67-68): narrow three-column captions wrap into awkward short lines; dimension annotations are low-contrast light green.

### Pagination, numbering and metadata
21. **Page count is wrong.** PDF 1-2: cover and contents say "page 1 of 74" although there are 75 physical PDF pages; viewer and printed numbering diverge after the cover.
22. **Empty and placeholder metadata.** PDF 1 and 6 (cover and 5): reviewer fields are empty, repeated placeholder-like metadata remains, and the software version is a product name rather than a version identifier.
23. **Sparse pages beside packed tables.** PDF 14, 28, 47-48, 64, 69, 74 (13, 27, 46-47, 63, 68, 73): pagination wastes substantial space while other tables are tightly packed.
24. **Weak typography.** Throughout: small light-green footer text has weak contrast; narrow headings break awkwardly (the web-philosophy header on PDF 10 and the Section 8.2 title on PDF 72).
25. **Standard editions disagree internally.** PDF 71-75 (70-74) versus 24 (23): IRC 22:2015 versus 2014, and IRC 112:2020 versus 2011. The correct editions need a separate authoritative check.

### Source portability
26. **Absolute image paths in the TeX.** Figures on PDF 3-4, 22-23 and 64-69: the TeX references absolute Windows temporary-directory image paths, so rebuilding the report standalone requires collecting and relinking the assets.
